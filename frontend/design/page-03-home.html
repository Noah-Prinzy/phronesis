Both kinds of navigation

§7.4 says "User never manually navigates" — the avatar reads intent and takes you there. I first built this with no tab bar at all to honour that literally. Overruled, and rightly.

Conversational routing stays the primary path: it is what makes this different from an app with a chatbot bolted on. The tab bar is the escape hatch — and it closes the one hole in the pure version, which is that nobody discovers a feature they never thought to ask for.

The thing to watch: if routing is unreliable, people give up asking and the tabs quietly become the only navigation. Then the thesis is dead in practice while still true on paper. Routing accuracy is what keeps this honest, not the absence of tabs.

The avatar is the microphone

Docking the avatar bottom-right put it exactly where a composer's mic button goes. Rather than have two controls that both mean "speak", the docked avatar is the mic — so the gesture you learn on Welcome is the gesture that works everywhere.

Once the tab bar took the bottom of the screen, a floating orb had nowhere honest to go: it either sat on the composer or on the conversation. So it moved into the composer, at the left of the line you are writing to it. Small, but always in thumb reach, and the composer then holds only what the avatar cannot be — attach, video, send.

Routing is offered, never taken

The avatar proposes a destination in the thread and waits. Silent navigation — where you type a sentence and the screen changes under you — is the fastest way to make an app feel like it is out of your control, especially when the intent match is wrong.

Press Routing to see it. The offer is a card in the conversation, not a modal, so declining costs nothing and the thread survives.

One thread, forever

§7.2 specifies one continuous thread per user, not separate conversations. So there is no chat list, no "new chat", no history drawer — those all imply segmentation the product does not have.

The chip beside the wordmark — 2015 Premio — is the small visible proof that it remembers. Memory the user cannot see is memory they will not trust. Account moved out of the app bar and into the tabs, so the same destination is never in two places.

Clarifying, not guessing

§7.3 gives the pattern: when intent is ambiguous, the avatar asks rather than picks. "Are you asking about a car you own, or one you're considering?" — with the answers as chips, because a two-way question deserves a two-tap answer.

Two open questions

Where does the journey toggle live? §3.2 lets users reach both journeys. If it is not a tab, it is either in Account or something the avatar handles conversationally — which is more consistent, but harder to find.

Does a routed page get a back arrow as well as a tab? If the avatar opens Diagnosis mid-conversation, returning to the thread is not the same gesture as switching sections. My instinct is yes: a back arrow for "undo that route", tabs for "go somewhere else".
