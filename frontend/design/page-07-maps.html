the rule

Nothing opaque covers the map

Every piece of chrome here — search, filters, the ETA pill, the zoom controls, the sheet — is glass. This is the one screen where the content is underneath the interface, so anything solid is hiding the thing the user opened the page for.

It is also the one screen where the glass cost is justified: a handful of small floating surfaces, not a scrolling list of them.

Two pin weights

The shop you are considering is a filled ember pin. Everything else is a hollow grey dot. On a map with a dozen candidates, rank has to be visible without reading a single label — and ember is the light in this system, so the recommended one is literally the lit one.

Your position is the only other marked thing: a soft ring, not a pin, because you are not a destination.

The route is downloaded

§10.9 asks for offline capability and this is where it matters most — the moment you need directions is often the moment the car has stopped somewhere with no signal.

So the sheet says so, before you set off rather than after you lose it. Promising offline behaviour only when it fails is how apps lose trust.

Filters carry the diagnosis

You did not arrive here by browsing — you arrived from a brake fault. So Brakes is already on, and the search field says what it searched for. The map opens with the question already asked.

needs your call

Which map, and what it costs

The map here is drawn, not real. In production this is Google Maps SDK (§6.2.10 names it) or an open alternative like MapLibre with OpenStreetMap tiles. Google gives traffic-aware ETA out of the box and bills per load; MapLibre is free and self-hosted but traffic is not included.

Either way it needs a dark style built to match this palette — the default dark themes are all blue-grey and will fight the ember. That is a real piece of work, not a config flag.
