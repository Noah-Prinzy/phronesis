why top-down

The car is a map, not a model

§6.2.5 asks for a 3D hologram you can orbit and zoom. On a phone, the job that view actually has to do is answer "where is the problem?" — and a top-down plan answers that instantly, with no camera to fight.

It is also what your own XPENG reference does. An orbitable model earns its place on the desktop layout, where there is room for it and a mouse to drive it.

Hotspots and findings are the same thing

Tap a marker on the car and its finding highlights; tap a finding and the marker pulses. A diagnostic view where the picture and the list are separate makes the reader do the joining, and they will get it wrong.

the rule paying off

Colour finally means something

This is the page the monochrome palette was built for. Everything is grey until something is wrong, so the two coloured markers on the car are the only saturated things on screen and cannot be missed.

Press Nothing wrong to see the other half of the argument: a clean scan has no colour at all. If "healthy" were green, this screen would be as loud as a critical fault.

Must-fix before nice-to-have

§6.2.6 asks for urgency ranking and a must-fix / nice-to-have split. Those are different questions, so the list groups by the second and sorts by the first — the thing that will strand you is at the top, and the thing that can wait is visibly below a line.

Confidence is shown per finding, as a bar and a number. A diagnosis that hides its own uncertainty is not a diagnosis, it is a guess with good posture.

Live data is a tab, not a dashboard

The OBD readings matter, but they are evidence, not the headline. They sit behind a tab so the findings get the screen — and the one reading that is out of range carries the only colour on that tab.

needs your call

Two things

No OBD paired. §8.3 says diagnosis still works from described symptoms, at lower confidence. That version of this page has no live tab and softer confidence numbers — worth designing explicitly, since plenty of users will never buy a reader.

Critical is not just a colour. If something means stop driving, a red badge in a list may not be enough. That may deserve an interrupt — the modal from the element sheet — rather than a row you can scroll past.
