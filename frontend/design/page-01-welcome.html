The gesture conflict

V1 said tap the avatar to continue. In the new system, tapping the avatar means microphone — on Home, on Account, everywhere. Teaching one meaning on the first screen and overriding it on the second is how an app ends up feeling arbitrary.

So Welcome gets a real button, and the avatar keeps its meaning intact. Tapping anywhere else still skips the reveal, which is what an impatient returning user actually wants.

Speaking, not animating

The words arrive in time with the voice rather than on a decorative timer, and the avatar sits in responding for exactly as long as there are words left. When it finishes, it drops to idle and the buttons rise.

In the app the same sequence is driven by the TTS audio, so the ring is reading the real waveform and the reveal tracks real speech. Here it runs on the fallback breath.

One action, not four

This screen exists to get you to the next one. A primary Get started, and a quiet route back for people who already have an account — nothing else competes.

No journey question here. That belongs in onboarding, where there is room to explain what the answer changes.

Copy

Rewritten from V1's three paragraphs, which opened with "I learn with every interaction" — true, but not something a person on their first screen has any reason to care about.

What replaced it names the three things the product actually does, and closes by telling you that you can type, speak, or show — because multi-modal input is the thing people least expect and most need to be told about. Revert it if you'd rather keep yours.

Still to settle

Language. The plan localises voice per country. If the UI localises too, this screen is where a language picker would live — and it is the one thing that might justify a fourth element.

Safe areas. The action buttons sit above a home-indicator gap; real devices need env(safe-area-inset-bottom).
