the whole point

Parts and labour, always split

§9.3 asks for an itemised parts-versus-labour breakdown, and it is the most useful thing on this page. A single number can only be accepted or refused. A split can be checked — you can price the pads yourself and see what you are paying for the hands.

So every price here carries the bar. It is the same grey twice at different weights, not two colours: this is information, not severity.

Cheapest is not first

§9.2 ranks on issue match, proximity, rating and cost together. The default sort is best match, not lowest price — for brakes, steering someone to the cheapest shop is not neutral, it is a safety decision made on their behalf.

Sort by price is one tap away. The ranking is a recommendation, and the user can always overrule it.

Two options, honestly compared

§9.4's report format offers alternative repairs, not one answer. Here that is the cheaper fix and the thorough one, each with what it does and does not solve.

One is marked recommended — the only ember on the page. If we are going to have an opinion, it should be visible and singular.

Quotes are requests, not prices

The figures shown are the industry range from the knowledge base. A shop's real number comes back after they see the car, and the copy says so — estimate, never price.

The primary action asks three shops at once, because getting three quotes is the single most effective thing a car owner can do and almost nobody does it.
